## 1. Score restructure (signal-routed-axes denominator)

**Source:** `docs/architectural-patterns.md` entry 8 — "AI-default overlap doesn't reflect deliberate signal."

**Problem.** Stage 4's `register_coherence_score` counts BOTH signal-routed direct matches AND AI-default fallback matches as overlap. Canonicals whose specs ARE the AI defaults (canonical-4 Standard SaaS Marketing is the worst offender) score a trivial 8/9 or 9/9 against any sparse brief because the brief's AI-default fallbacks "match" the canonical's AI-default specs. The lenient overlap is a fill-eligibility signal, not a "this is the right canonical" signal — but v0.1's conflation of the two means trivial-fill 1.0 confidences appear on briefs that should route as novel.

**v0.2 design.** Restructure `register_coherence_score` to use `directMatchCount / signalRoutedAxesCount` rather than `(directMatch + AI-default-match) / 9`. Denominate by the axes the brief actually signaled, so canonicals that ride on AI-default-overlap don't get rewarded for "matching" axes the brief never spoke to.

**Worked example.** `novel-saas-quiet-authority`: brief signal-routes 2 axes (LAYOUT-1, COLOR-8a). Under v0.2 score:
- canonical-1's directMatchCount=1 (LAYOUT) → 1/2 = 0.5
- canonical-4's directMatchCount=1 (COLOR) → 1/2 = 0.5
- Both score 0.5; neither hits the 6/9 register-coherence threshold; Stage 5 doesn't fill from either; brief routes as novel. Correct.

**Downstream threshold recalibration required.** Implementing this changes the score distribution shape:
- `REGISTER_COHERENCE_AXIS_THRESHOLD` (currently 6/9 ≈ 0.667 on the v0.1 score) — need a new threshold on the v0.2 score (probably ~0.6 of `directMatchCount / signalRoutedAxesCount`).
- `min_confidence` floor in fixtures (currently 0.85 for canonical fixtures) — need fixture-level recalibration; some fixtures may need their floors adjusted.
- `MAX_AI_DEFAULT_FILL_THRESHOLD` (currently 2 axes) — may need re-tuning since fill semantics change.
- `CONTRADICTION_TIE_RATIO` and `CONTRADICTION_MAX_REGISTER_OVERLAP` (Phase 2.1a Task 2 contradictory-canonicals detector) — fires on top-1/top-2 score ratio, which changes shape.

**Expected closures from this item alone:**

- `novel-saas-quiet-authority` (was a Task 6a regression that's now passing via baseline preservation; this v0.2 score restructure is what cleanly closes it without the iteration-order accident)
- `editorial-magazine-intent`, `cause-journalism-intent`, `editorial-brand-hybrid-intent` (sparse intent-path briefs that currently route to canonical-1 via AI-default collapse) — should route as novel or surface routing_ambiguous correctly
- `plain-document-vibes` (top-3 tied at 1.0 directMatch=1) — should route correctly with score below threshold
- `novel-developer-tool-editorial`, `novel-luxury-hotel-scrollytelling` (Cat D fixtures Task 7 was scoped for) — should route as novel without needing a dedicated detector

Approximate count: 4-7 fixture closures from item 1 alone.

**Estimated v0.2 work:** 4-8h (implementation + fixture recalibration + testing). Significant scope because of downstream threshold cascading.

## 2. Whole-phrase matching for brand-exemplar altnames + surface-specific altnames

**Source:** `docs/architectural-patterns.md` entry 7 — "Brand-surface conflation: brands operate across multiple surfaces with different design vocabularies."

**Problem.** Stage 2 matches signals against altname phrases via substring. Multi-surface brands (Stripe, Apple, Linear, others) appear in altnames across multiple grammars per axis, each tagged with a specific surface (e.g., `like Apple's iOS` in RP-6 vs `like Apple's gradient pages` in COLOR-7). When a brief mentions just `Apple` (intended generically as iPhone marketing), the substring match hits ALL grammars with `Apple` in any altname — producing Stage 2 ties at conf 0.9 across multiple grammars on each axis. The bare brand mention floods routing rather than disambiguating it.

**v0.2 design.** Stage 2 matching changes from substring to whole-phrase. The bare signal `Apple` no longer matches `like Apple's iOS` (different phrase) — only signals that contain the full phrase do. Multi-surface brands then get explicit surface-tagged altnames, with bare-brand variants in surface-default grammars.

**Implementation steps:**

1. Stage 2 matching algorithm: tokenize signal and altname phrase, require all altname tokens (or N-1) to appear in signal. (Exact rule TBD during implementation; test with corpus to find the right tokenization granularity.)
2. Audit all multi-surface brand altnames in the corpus. Add surface qualifiers where missing (`like Apple's iPhone marketing` to COLOR-7 / IMG-?), add bare-brand variants to surface-default grammars (`like Apple` to a marketing-surface grammar set).
3. Update the Stripe and Apple cataloging in `docs/altname-coverage-notes.md` entries 13 and 14 to reflect the v0.2 altname additions.

**Expected closures:**

- `premium-hardware-brand` (Apple/RP-6 conflation — see coverage-notes entry 7 + 14) — closes when bare `Apple` no longer substring-matches `like Apple's iOS`
- `saas-marketing-brand` (Stripe-fanout on imagery + reading_pattern — entries 13 + Task 6c triage Type C rows) — closes when bare `Stripe` no longer substring-matches surface-specific altnames
- Various other brand-fanout fixtures surfaced in `docs/task-6c-triage-table.md` Type C rows

Approximate count: 3-5 fixture closures from item 2.

**Estimated v0.2 work:** 6-10h (Stage 2 redesign + corpus altname audit + testing).

## 3. Signal-level disambiguation for register-adjacent canonicals

**Source:** `docs/architectural-patterns.md` entry 2 (Contradiction detection on signals not scores) + entry 9 (register-adjacency).

**Problem.** Briefs that signal-distinct register intents (e.g., "essay style + engaged committed reader + long-form" vs "documentary + photojournalism + cause-driven") collapse into the same lenient overlap score because both invoke editorial-axis grammars that multiple canonicals share. Stage 4's score-level matching can't see the per-signal canonical attribution distinction.

**v0.2 design.** Per-signal canonical-attribution layer that operates BEFORE Stage 4's score collapse. Each signal in the brief is attributed to one or more canonicals via its altname provenance; Stage 4 uses the per-canonical signal-attribution distribution as additional input to the score, not just the per-axis grammar match.

**Implementation steps:**

1. Add canonical-attribution to altname structure: each altname entry tags which canonical(s) it belongs to.
2. Stage 1 / Stage 2 outputs gain per-signal canonical-attribution.
3. Stage 4 uses the per-canonical attribution distribution to adjust scores (canonicals invoked by more signals score higher; canonicals invoked by zero attributions score lower regardless of axis-level match).

**Expected closures:**

- `editorial-longform-vibes` (canonical-10 vs canonical-14 register-adjacency — entry 11 in coverage-notes) — closes when essay-style signals attribute to canonical-10 specifically, journalism signals to canonical-14
- `cause-journalism-vibes` (canonical-14 vs canonical-2/10 — entry 12) — closes if Mission-Earnest is also resolved (item 4)
- `editorial-magazine-vibes` motion FAIL (Task 6c triage Type A — `member's-club energy` should attribute to canonical-2 over other editorial canonicals)

Approximate count: 1-3 fixture closures from item 3 alone.

**Estimated v0.2 work:** 8-12h (architecture redesign — significant; depends on item 1 landing first since signal-attribution has to integrate with the new score).

## 4. Mission-Earnest grammar resolution

**Problem.** Mission-Earnest is in the corpus loader's `KNOWN_UNRESOLVABLE` allowlist — a spec annotation in canonical-14 (Cause / Journalism / Mission-Driven) that doesn't load as a discrete grammar. Path A is cleaner architecturally (Mission-Earnest IS a distinct register worth its own grammar); Path B is the cheaper tactical fix.

**Expected closures:**

### 8. Phase 3 Task 2 token-grammar mapping bug (corrected scope)

**Source:** `docs/phase3-integration-results.md` Brief 1 finding A; Phase 3 close audit revealed substantially larger scope than the initial framing suggested.

**Initial framing (rejected after audit):** "doc-only comment drift, ~1-2h cleanup."

**Actual scope:** 14 drift instances across `src/cards/tokens/{color,typography,spacing,radius}.ts`. Pattern includes both comment-only drift AND value-vs-grammar-ID swaps. Integration test produced reasonable output anyway because (a) engine routes by ID, (b) some grammars' values happen to fit briefs by content alignment, but briefs routing to swapped grammars get systematically-wrong tokens.

**Full drift list (audit ran at Phase 3 close, commit `caa1d87`-base):**

```
COLOR.TS — 6 drift instances (out of 9 grammars)
  COLOR-1: comment "Earth-Pulled Restraint"          — corpus "Three-Color Discipline (Paper / Ink / Stone)"
  COLOR-2: comment "Two-Color Monochrome"            — corpus "Earth-Pulled Restraint"
  COLOR-3: comment "Restrained-Editorial-Earth"      — corpus "Two-Color Monochrome"
  COLOR-5: comment "Aggressive-Saturated"            — corpus "Saturated-Primary (Neo-Brutalist)"
  COLOR-6: comment "Warm-Friendly DTC"               — corpus "Pastel-Vibrant (Y2K and Dopamine)"
  COLOR-7: comment "Iridescent / Saturated-Primary"  — corpus "Iridescent / Gradient-Tech"

TYPOGRAPHY.TS — 2 drift instances (value-vs-ID swap)
  TYPE-4: comment "Maximalist-Expressive"   — corpus "Mono-Discipline (Terminal-Coded)"
  TYPE-6: comment "Mono-Discipline"         — corpus "Maximalist-Expressive (Anti-Type-Hierarchy)"

SPACING.TS (Density) — 4 drift instances (value-vs-ID swap)
  DEN-2: comment "Premium-Spaced"          — corpus "Marketing-Airy (Premium-Spacious)"
  DEN-4: comment "Application-Density"     — corpus "Marketing-Dense (Enterprise-Serious)"
  DEN-5: comment "Hyper-Dense"             — corpus "Application-Dense (Power-User UI)"
  DEN-6: comment "Marketing-Airy"          — corpus "Hyper-Dense (Information-Maximalist)"

RADIUS.TS (Component) — 2 drift instances (value-vs-ID swap)
  COMP-6: comment "Premium-Refined"        — corpus "Glass / Layered (Translucent-Atmospheric)"
  COMP-7: comment "Glass / Layered"        — corpus "Maximalist-Decorative"

MOTION.TS — clean, 0 drift
```

**Diagnosis.** Three drift shapes coexist:

- **Pure rename** (e.g., COLOR-7 "Iridescent / Saturated-Primary" → "Iridescent / Gradient-Tech"): comment is wrong but values still fit the corpus name. Comment-only fix.
- **Value-vs-ID swap** (TYPE-4 ↔ TYPE-6, DEN-5 ↔ DEN-6, COMP-6 ↔ COMP-7): values authored for grammar X were assigned to grammar Y's ID. My TYPE-4 token values (Recoleta serif display, Inter body) are spec-correct for "Maximalist-Expressive" — the corpus calls that grammar TYPE-6. Values need to MOVE between IDs.
- **Different register, similar values** (COLOR-1, COLOR-2, COLOR-3): my COLOR-1 values (warm-earth canvas + accent) are spec-correct for what the corpus calls COLOR-2 (Earth-Pulled Restraint). The grammars have different defining properties (Three-Color Discipline vs Earth-Pulled Restraint vs Two-Color Monochrome) but my token tables conflated them onto adjacent register-shapes.

**Why integration test still produced reasonable output:** routing happens by ID, not name. When Brief 1 ("luxury hotel like Aman, warm-earth photography") routed COLOR-3, the engine selected the ID that altname-substring-matched best, and my COLOR-3 token table happened to ship warm-earth values regardless of what the corpus calls COLOR-3. Functional output for THAT brief was fine. Briefs that route to swapped grammars (e.g., a brief that should hit COLOR-1 "Three-Color Discipline" but my values for COLOR-1 are warm-earth-Earth-Pulled) get systematically-wrong tokens.

**v0.2 fix path:**

1. Determine authoritative grammar names per ID by reading corpus directly (not from my Phase 3 comments).
2. For each token-table entry, classify: pure rename (fix comment only) vs value-vs-ID swap (move values to correct ID) vs different-register conflation (re-author values to match corpus's actual grammar definition).
3. Apply per-ID fixes. For value-vs-ID swaps, this is a pair-wise move (TYPE-4's values become TYPE-6, TYPE-6's become TYPE-4 — and vice versa).
4. Re-run `docs/phase3-integration-runbook.md` 6-brief integration test to confirm output quality is preserved or improved.
5. Add a verification script (`scripts/verify-token-table-comments.ts`) that diffs comments against the corpus's grammar names and fails CI on drift.

**Expected closures:** 0 fixture closures directly. Improves real-world correctness for briefs that route to currently-swapped grammar IDs (the integration test sample is too small to detect this; corpus-wide validation would).

**Estimated v0.2 work:** 2-4h focused (audit + classification + per-ID fixes + integration-test re-validation + verification script). Moderate priority — doesn't block v0.1 ship, but should land before any v0.1.x release that depends on token-axis correctness or before v0.2 work that builds on token tables.

**Author note (Phase 3 close):** The initial framing as "doc-only" was wrong. Audit at phase close revealed the value-vs-ID swap pattern. Recording the corrected scope here so v0.2 implementer doesn't re-derive the audit.

### 9. Card self-contradiction detection

**Source:** `docs/phase3-integration-results.md` Brief 3 finding B.

**Problem.** When the routed grammar matches a pattern that the matched canonical's `anti_patterns` list says to avoid, the card output contradicts itself. Brief 3 demonstrated the failure: card's `anti_patterns` says "Avoid the purple-to-blue gradient hero" while card's `tokens.color` ships exactly that gradient palette. The consuming AI tool sees the contradiction and currently has to detect it manually.

**v0.2 design.** Post-processing pass in the card generator (`src/cards/generate.ts`) that:

1. Walks `anti_patterns` strings.
2. For each pattern, checks whether the card's `tokens` / `axes` / `components` content matches the pattern's "avoid X" referent. Pattern-detection rules:
   - "purple-to-blue gradient" → check `tokens.color` for purple (#7-9*) and blue (#3-6*) accents in `accent.primary` / `accent.secondary` / `bg.canvas`
   - "rounded-2xl" → check `tokens.radius.lg` ≥ "16px"
   - "centered-hero / three-column-features" → check routed `axes.layout.grammar_id` === "LAYOUT-4a"
   - "Inter everywhere" → check `tokens.typography.font_family.display` and `body` both contain "Inter"
3. Where a match fires, emit a `card_self_contradiction` `open_warning` with severity:warning, naming the specific contradiction.

**Expected closures:** 0 fixture closures (no validation suite test currently checks for this). Improves consumer UX: AI tools see the contradiction explicitly and can ask the user to clarify rather than generating self-contradicting code.

**Estimated v0.2 work:** 4-6h (pattern-matching helpers + post-processing pass + tests + integration with existing warning surface).

### 10. Sparse-vague brief routing imprecision

**Source:** `docs/phase3-integration-results.md` Brief 6 finding C.

**Problem.** Briefs with 2-3 vague signals (above the 2-signal density gate but below confident-routing threshold) route to plausible-but-imprecise canonicals. Brief 6 demonstrated: "real estate brand called Lotusiya, make it chic" routed to canonical-10 (Editorial Long-Form / Substack / Stripe Press) when the natural-fit canonical was canonical-1 (Luxury Hospitality) or canonical-12 (Premium Editorial-Brand Hybrid).

**v0.2 design (overlap with item 1 + 3):**

- Item 1's score restructure (signal-routed-axes denominator) makes vague-brief routing self-evident: when only 2-3 axes are signal-routed, `register_coherence_score = directMatchCount / 3` produces values well below the confidence threshold, and the engine surfaces a low-confidence routing rather than committing to a plausible-imprecise canonical.
- Item 3's signal-level disambiguation (per architectural-patterns entry 9) further refines: "chic" + "real estate" should attribute differently to luxury-hospitality canonicals vs editorial canonicals, even at low signal density.

**Interim mitigation (v0.1.x patch, optional):** add a `metadata.confidence_interpretation` field that carries a human-readable hint when min-of-axes is below 0.7 — "Card routed at moderate confidence; brief was sparse. Consider asking user for brand exemplars or surface qualifiers." Consuming AI tools surface this to users.

**Expected closures:** 0 fixture closures directly (the validation suite doesn't have "sparse vague" fixtures). Improves real-world UX significantly — vague briefs are the most common real-world brief shape per integration test.

**Estimated v0.2 work:** Mostly subsumed by items 1 + 3 (~0h standalone). Optional v0.1.x patch: ~1h (one new field + render logic).

## v0.1 design properties to preserve in v0.2

Three properties emerged from Phase 3's integration test that are working-as-designed, load-bearing for current behavior, and worth holding as constraints when v0.2 work touches the affected layers. These are NOT items to fix — they're things v0.2 should not break.

### Property 1: Token-vs-grammar coupling stays loose

**Source:** `docs/phase3-integration-results.md` Brief 1 observation.

**The pattern.** When the routing engine selects a grammar whose canonical-axis-mapping isn't strictly aligned with the brief's intent, the per-grammar token table can still produce correct user-facing output IF the routed grammar's tokens happen to align with the brief's intent. Brief 1 demonstrated: TYPE-4 routed (off-canonical for Luxury Hospitality), but the token values shipped (Recoleta serif display) were correct for the brief's "serif display type" intent.

**Why it matters.** v0.1's deterministic Stage 1 has limited recall on creative paraphrasing. The token-vs-grammar loose coupling is a robustness pattern that lets imprecise routing still produce usable output. If v0.2's LLM Stage 1 makes routing more precise, token tables should NOT be tightened to require grammar-id-exact alignment with brief intent — that would make v0.2 less tolerant of routing imprecision than v0.1, regression in robustness even as precision improves.

**v0.2 design constraint.** When restructuring scoring (item 1) or refining altname matching (item 2), do NOT add a "grammar-id must match brief intent" check that would override the token-vs-grammar loose coupling. The looseness is a feature; preserve it.

### Property 2: Internal coherence beats strict canonical alignment

**Source:** `docs/phase3-integration-results.md` Brief 4 observation.

**The pattern.** Brief 4 routed canonical-4 (Standard SaaS Marketing) but voice routed VOICE-3 (Friendly Expert via Stripe support fanout) instead of canonical-4's primary VOICE-7 (Conversion-Punchy). The card's `voice_guidelines` reflected VOICE-3's directives consistently throughout — didn't mix VOICE-3 "be helpful" advice with VOICE-7 "ship faster" advice. Internal coherence is what made the partial-pass actually usable for the consuming AI tool.

**Why it matters.** Strict canonical alignment (force voice to canonical's primary even when signal-routing prefers a fan-out alternative) would make routing more "correct" by canonical lookup but would break internal coherence — the card's voice_guidelines would describe VOICE-7 directives while other axes are routed via canonical-4 fanout to different grammars. Mixed-grammar guidance is worse output than coherent-but-fanned-out guidance.

**v0.2 design constraint.** When v0.2's whole-phrase matching (item 2) reduces brand-fanout, internal coherence should stay preserved. Don't introduce a "force voice to canonical primary" override that creates mixed-grammar guidance. The card's directives across axes should reference the routed grammars consistently — even when one or two axes deviate from canonical primaries.

### Property 3: Dual-confidence signal as calibration channel

**Source:** `docs/phase3-integration-results.md` Brief 6 observation.

**The pattern.** Brief 6 returned `canonical_match_confidence: 1.0` (route-level claim of exact canonical match) AND `metadata.confidence: 0.6` (per-axis min-of-axes rollup). The discrepancy is honest signaling — the engine asserts a strong canonical alignment AT THE OVERLAP LEVEL while reporting that per-axis routing has weak confidences. Consuming AI tools that trust min-of-axes get accurate calibration: "this card is moderately trustworthy; ask the user to refine the brief."

**Why it matters.** v0.1's deterministic Stage 1 can't differentiate between "user gave us a sparse-but-vague brief" and "user gave us a sparse-but-precise brief." The dual-confidence signal IS the differentiation channel — when canonical_match_confidence is high but min-of-axes is low, the brief was vague enough that routing snapped to the nearest canonical at lenient overlap but couldn't get strong per-axis evidence.

**v0.2 design constraint.** When v0.2 LLM Stage 1 (coverage-notes entry 16's path) extracts more signals from the same vague brief, the dual-confidence signal should stay. Briefs that are genuinely ambiguous — even with LLM extraction — still need to surface "here's what I'd guess, but ask the user" UX. Removing the per-axis min-of-axes rollup in favor of a single canonical confidence would lose the calibration channel.

### Why these are properties, not items

The v0.2 backlog items 1-10 are FIXES — work that closes documented gaps. These three properties are CONSTRAINTS — invariants v0.2 design must preserve. The distinction matters: a v0.2 implementer should be able to read items 1-10 and decide what to build; reading these three properties tells them what NOT to break while building.
